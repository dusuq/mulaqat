# Outreach playbook

Tone: peer to peer, plain English, short lines. No "I hope this finds you well", no buzzwords, at most one emoji.
Every message names something specific to the person. If you can't find anything specific, ask the user for a detail instead of sending something generic.

## Sequence and timing

| Step                | When                 | Goal                                  | Length                    |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Connection note     | Day 0                | Get accepted                          | ≤200 chars (hard max 300) |
| First message       | Day 1–3 after accept | Start a conversation about their pain | ≤600 chars                |
| Follow-up 1         | Day 7 if no reply    | Give something useful                 | ≤500 chars                |
| Follow-up 2 (final) | Day 14 if no reply   | Easy out, leave the door open         | ≤300 chars                |

After follow-up 2, stop. Set the lead to `lost` only if they said no; otherwise leave it at `messaged` with no next date.

## Connection note

- Reference their post, company news, role or a shared context.
- No pitch, no link, no "I'd love to pick your brain".

> Saw your post on handling Eid order spikes at the store, Sara — the bit about WhatsApp queues hit home. Would be good to connect.

## First message

Thank them, one observation about their business, one question about the pain. No link yet.

> Thanks for connecting, Sara. Noticed you're taking orders on WhatsApp and Instagram at the same time. Curious — when a sale is on, how long do customers usually wait for a reply?

## Follow-up 1 — give something

A tip, a 2-line idea for their case, or a real result from a similar client.

> One thing that's worked for stores your size: an auto-reply that answers stock and delivery questions and hands the rest to a person. One client cut first-reply time from hours to under a minute. Happy to sketch how it'd look for you.

## Follow-up 2 — easy out

> Totally fine if this isn't a priority right now — happy to stay connected. If reply times ever become a headache, I'm around.

## Comments on prospects' posts

Add something real: a specific agreement with a reason, a short experience, or a sharp question. 1–3 sentences. Never pitch in comments.

## Don'ts

- Same text to two people
- Links in the connection note or first message
- Guilt-trip follow-ups ("Just bumping this up…")
- Claims you can't back with a real result
